## Editing and Generation in One Workflow

Grammarly began as a writing-assistance product and has expanded from corrections into style suggestions and generative features. In the reviewed setup, it analyzed:

- **Correctness** — grammar, spelling, punctuation (the table stakes)
- **Clarity** — wordiness, passive voice, unclear antecedents, confusing sentence structure
- **Engagement** — monotonous sentence patterns, weak word choices, repetitive phrasing
- **Delivery** — tone detection (confident, friendly, formal, etc.), audience-appropriate language
- **Plagiarism** — checks against 16 billion web pages and academic databases (Premium)

The evaluation referred to GrammarlyGO for tone rewrites, prompt-based drafting, brainstorming and suggested replies. Feature names and availability can change; consult the current vendor plan page for what is included.

### Test 1: Finding Deliberate Errors

**Test:** A 2,000-word document with 50 intentionally planted errors — 25 grammar (subject-verb agreement, comma splices, dangling modifiers, etc.), 15 spelling (including homophone errors like "their/there"), and 10 punctuation errors.

**Grammarly Premium:** Identified 47 of 50 planted errors, a 94% detection rate. It missed a comma splice, a homophone in quoted text and an ambiguous dangling modifier. It also offered two optional clarity suggestions on correct sentences. Its explanations were clear.

**ChatGPT:** Found 38 of the 50 errors (76%). It missed the “affect/effect” and “complement/compliment” homophones and was less consistent on punctuation in this test.

<div class="verdict-box">
  <div class="verdict-label">📝 Verdict</div>
  <p class="verdict-text">
    <strong>Winner: Grammarly — 94% vs 76% detection.</strong> ChatGPT can check grammar, but Grammarly's specialized model catches significantly more errors, especially the subtle ones. For professional editing: Grammarly is meaningfully better.
  </p>
</div>

### Test 2: Adjusting Tone and Clarity

**Task:** A business email draft that reads as passive-aggressive ("As I mentioned in my previous email... I'm not sure if you had a chance to review..."). Goal: make it sound helpful and collaborative.

**Grammarly Premium:** Labeled the draft “disapproving” and “accusatory,” and offered alternatives such as “Following up on” and “When you have a moment.” The revised email sounded more collaborative while retaining its facts. Grammarly also recommended splitting a 32-word sentence.

**ChatGPT:** Also produced a more collaborative rewrite, which the reviewer found natural. It required moving the text to and from a separate chat, while Grammarly’s suggestions appeared inline in the tested email composer.

<div class="verdict-box">
  <div class="verdict-label">📝 Verdict</div>
  <p class="verdict-text">
    <strong>Grammarly for workflow; ChatGPT for deeper rewrites.</strong> Grammarly's inline tone detection catches problems as you write. ChatGPT can do deeper structural rewrites when you need more than tone adjustment. Use both — Grammarly for real-time feedback, ChatGPT for major rewrites.
  </p>
</div>

### Test 3: Drafting and Rewriting

**Task:** Use GrammarlyGO to: (a) generate a 200-word product announcement from bullet points, (b) rewrite a paragraph in three different tones, (c) brainstorm 10 blog post titles on a given topic.

**GrammarlyGO:** The announcement was clear, professional and faithful to the bullet points, though more formulaic than ChatGPT’s. Its professional, friendly and persuasive rewrites were distinct. The 10 suggested titles ranged from inventive to generic.

The generation features were convenient when already editing in Grammarly. For standalone drafting, the original review preferred ChatGPT and Claude.

<div class="verdict-box">

  </p>

### Test 4: Using Grammarly Across Apps

**Test:** Write and edit across Google Docs (article draft), Gmail (pitch email), Slack (team message), LinkedIn (post), and a web form (contact page). Track whether Grammarly consistently catches errors.

**Grammarly Premium:** In the test, the browser extension handled Gmail, web Slack and LinkedIn; Google Docs showed inline suggestions; and the web form displayed Grammarly’s widget. No copy-and-paste step was needed on these five services.

**ChatGPT/Claude:** The tested workflow involved copying text into a separate tab and back. The reviewer found that extra step less convenient for quick corrections.

For writers who work across multiple platforms daily, this alone justifies the subscription.
